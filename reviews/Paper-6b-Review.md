## Critical Findings

### C1. The T_⊙ ≈ T_CMB "match" is circular by construction, and the supporting table arithmetic is wrong anyway
- **Location:** Abstract (lines 58–60), §3.6 Table 2 (lines 386–410), §6.1 (lines 684–713), Prediction 4 (lines 851–854).
- **Claim:** "The local Aether temperature at the Sun's position (~2.75 K) matches T_CMB to within 1%" — presented as an observational consistency result ("This near-equality is not a coincidence," line 699).
- **Why wrong (circularity):** Line 387–388 states the profile is constructed by "anchoring the halo edge to T_CMB = 2.725 K." The entire galactic profile spans only 0.053 K (the paper's own Table 2). Anchor + tiny gradient ⇒ every point in the galaxy is within ~2% of T_CMB *by assumption*. Nothing is tested. The only physics content would be an independent derivation of T_wall from the viscous-heating/cooling balance; §6.1 offers only a qualitative sentence ("both of which depend on H(t) and the age of the universe"). Worse, the model's own dynamics contradict the "not a coincidence" claim: §4.2 has T_wall ≈ const while T_CMB ∝ (1+z). Two quantities with different time dependence can match only at one epoch — this is precisely a "why now" coincidence, the very problem Paper 6(a) was written to dissolve.
- **Evidence (arithmetic):** With the stated profile T(r) = 2.725 + A·ln(200 kpc/r), A = 2v_flat²/(c²η_K) = 0.01007 K: T(8 kpc) = **2.757 K** (table says 2.747), T(15 kpc) = **2.751** (table: 2.742), T(50 kpc) = **2.739** (table: 2.733). The interior entries fit *no* logarithmic profile consistent with the endpoints (implied A varies from 0.0115 to 0.0149 across rows). Correct solar offset: **32.4 mK = 1.19%**, not "22 mK, 0.8%" (lines 696–697). The abstract's "within 1%" fails on the corrected arithmetic.
- **Fix direction:** Either derive T_wall independently from the heating balance and *then* compare to T_CMB, or reframe §6.1 as an assumption ("we set T_wall = T_CMB") and delete the "not a coincidence" paragraph and Prediction 4's "would falsify" language. Recompute Table 2 from the stated profile (2.778 / 2.778 / 2.757 / 2.751 / 2.739 / 2.725) and propagate to the Δc/c column and §6.1 numbers.

### C2. "Dark energy is absent at z > 2" is contradicted by the paper's own equations: the reversed contrast grows without bound

- **Fix direction:** Either (a) introduce and justify a saturation/cutoff of the linear K_A(T) form (which then must be reconciled with the He-4 anchor and with the z_rev arithmetic), or (b) honestly compute the reversed-epoch dynamics and confront them with high-z structure growth and CMB constraints. Prediction 3 must be rewritten: the model does not predict *absence* of dark energy at z > 2, it predicts sign-reversed dark energy growing with z.

### M1. The helium-4 anchor misrepresents real He-4 first-sound data
- **Location:** §1 item 3 (lines 110–114), §2.3 (lines 196–201), Table 1 caption (lines 332–334), Prediction 1 (lines 834–838).
- **Claim:** "The fractional sensitivity at low temperature (T < 1 K) is approximately (1/c₁²)|dc₁²/dT| ~ 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹"; benchmark η_K = 1.07×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ "matches this value exactly"; cold superfluid is universally stiffer.
- **Why wrong:** Real He-4 (Donnelly & Barenghi 1998 reference data): u₁ ≈ 238.3 m/s at T → 0 and is *constant to a few parts in 10⁴ below 1 K*, with a weak extremum near ~0.5–0.7 K where du₁/dT changes sign — so in exactly the model's void-temperature range (T_void,0 = 0.45–0.91 K), He-4's sensitivity is (i) not a single well-defined number (it varies strongly and passes through zero at the extremum) and (ii) *not uniformly of the required sign* ("colder ⇒ stiffer" fails below the extremum). *(Revised 2026-07-07 by verification pass: the original point (i) — "far below any clean 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ over most of the range" — overstated the magnitude discrepancy; the range-averaged |d ln c₁²/dT| below 1 K is itself of order a few ×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹, so the paper's order-of-magnitude figure is defensible as an average. The finding stands on the sign non-uniformity in the void range, the spurious "matches exactly," the T_λ problem, and the linearity mismatch.)* Between 1 K and T_λ the sensitivity rises steeply, reaching |d ln c₁²/dT| ~ 10⁻²–10⁻¹ K⁻¹ near 2 K (u₁ falls from ~238 to ~217–220 m/s approaching T_λ). There is no single "measured temperature sensitivity of helium-4 at low temperature"; it spans ≥3 orders of magnitude and both signs. Claiming a *fitted* value of 1.07×10⁻⁴ (see M2) "matches exactly" an order-of-magnitude figure is spurious precision. Additionally, T_wall = 2.725 K exceeds T_λ = 2.17 K: in the analogy, matter-containing regions sit in the *normal* (He I) phase, outside the superfluid regime the whole paper leans on. Finally, the linear constant-η_K form Eq. (KA-T) contradicts He-4's strongly nonlinear K(T).
- **Fix direction:** State the He-4 comparison as loose qualitative motivation only (above ~1 K, colder He II is stiffer); quote the actual sensitivity range with temperature windows; delete "matches this value exactly"; note the T_λ problem explicitly or rescale the analogy (as 6(a) does for the roton gap: "need not equal helium's value").

### M2. η_K is fitted, not doubly constrained: the "single parameter governs both phenomena" claim is vacuous

- **Claim:** "Both the dark matter constraint ... and the dark energy constraint ... are satisfied by the same η_K" (lines 466–468); "Independent measurements of both should converge on the same value — a powerful consistency test" (Prediction 6).
- **Why wrong:** The paper itself states (line 309) that the dark matter result ΔK_A/K_A = 5.7×10⁻⁶ "is *independent of η_K*" — the DM sector places **no constraint whatsoever** on η_K; any value in Table 1 works (that is the stated point of the table's "degeneracy"). η_K is fixed entirely by one chain: assume z_cross = 0.5 and β = 1 → T_void,0 = T_wall/3 = 0.908 K → ΔT_cosmic = 1.82 K → η_K = 1.95×10⁻⁴/1.82 = 1.07×10⁻⁴. One equation, one unknown: a fit, with the He-4 "match" then declared post hoc (see M1). There are no two independent determinations to "converge," so Prediction 6 is empty as constructed, and Prediction 1 is a fitted value relabeled a prediction.
- **Fix direction:** Present η_K as a single-fit parameter from the DE sector; the honest consistency statement is only that the fitted value lands within He-4's (broad) sensitivity range. A real two-sided test would require an independent handle on ΔT_halo (e.g., from the heating-rate calculation of Open Question 7).

### M3. The β = 3 parameter column is internally inconsistent (wrong z_rev; Eq. Tvoid-z contradicts Eq. Tvoid-evolution)
- **Location:** §4.2 Eqs. (Tvoid-evolution, line 483) and (Tvoid-z, line 511); §4.3 Eq. (z-reversal, line 530); §5.2 Table 4 (lines 629–647).
- **Claim:** For β = 3: T_void,0 = 0.45 K, z_rev = 5.1.
- **Why wrong:** Eq. (Tvoid-evolution) dT/dt = −βHT integrates to T_void ∝ (1+z)^β, but Eq. (Tvoid-z) asserts exponent 1 — the two are consistent only for β = 1. The DE-magnitude equation (η_K = 8.6×10⁻⁵ column entry) is arithmetically fine, but the column's cosmology is not the one its own β implies.

### M4. Internal contradiction about what the CMB is: "directly measures the Aether temperature" vs. "photons do not thermalize with the Aether"
- **Location:** §6.1 (lines 686–689, quoting Paper 6), §6.2 (lines 716–728), Prediction 4 (lines 851–854).
- **Claim:** Prediction 4: "The CMB temperature directly measures the Aether temperature in mass-containing regions." §6.2: CMB photons "propagate through the local Aether without thermalizing: direct photon–quasiparticle scattering is suppressed by P_th/K_A ~ 10⁻³⁸."
- **Why wrong:** These cannot both hold. If instead the CMB is the aether's local thermal spectrum (Paper 6's identification, endorsed in §6.1), the warm-wall/cold-void structure (ΔT ≈ 1.8 K between walls and void centers, per this paper) should imprint enormous anisotropy as sightlines traverse voids — δT/T ~ 0.6, not 10⁻⁵. - **Fix direction:** Pick one ontology for the CMB and carry it through: either drop Prediction 4 and §6.1's identification (treating T_wall ≈ T_CMB as coincidence/assumption), or explain quantitatively how a non-interacting photon field nonetheless tracks the aether temperature.

### M5. No transport mechanism can establish or maintain the required temperature profiles; the "natural steady-state" log profile is asserted, not derived
- **Location:** §3.2 (lines 290–293), §3.6, Open Question 2 (lines 881–885); interacts with §7.4 Bullet Cluster (lines 770–782).
- **Claim:** "A logarithmic temperature profile is the natural steady-state solution for viscous heating from a central gravitational flow source in a medium with constant thermal conductivity."
- **Why wrong / unsupported:** (i) For a *central point source* in 3D spherical steady state, T ∝ 1/r, not ln r; the log profile requires *distributed* heating ε̇ ∝ 1/r² plus constant conductivity — neither is established (the heating profile depends on the unspecified v(r) of the inflow and the strongly T-dependent viscosity the paper itself invokes in §2.2). (ii) Timescale: reaching any steady state across a 200 kpc halo within the age of the universe requires effective thermal diffusivity D ≳ L²/t_H ≈ **9×10²⁵ m²/s** (recomputed) — an effective sustained transport speed ≳10⁴ m/s over kpc scales. No aether thermal conductivity or transport channel (second sound? counterflow?) is ever quantified; if aether heat transport is slow, the profile can never be established; if fast enough, the wall–void contrast of §4 (which requires voids *not* to be reheated by adjacent warm walls over Gyr) is threatened. The two sections implicitly demand opposite transport regimes at different scales, and neither is calculated. (iii) The Bullet Cluster explanation additionally requires each galaxy's profile to translate with it at ~4700 km/s on ~10⁸ yr encounter timescales — incompatible with a profile that takes ~Gyr to build.
- **Fix direction:** Specify the aether's thermal conductivity/transport mechanism, verify the steady-state assumption and the log form from the actual ε̇(r), and check that the same conductivity simultaneously permits the galactic steady state and forbids wall→void equilibration. This is the single largest physics gap beneath the DM half of the model.
