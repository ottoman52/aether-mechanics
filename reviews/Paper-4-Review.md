## Major Findings

### M1. §2.5 Self-Sustained Pumps — species universality of Coulomb strength is claimed as established; the medium clock fixes only frequency, not amplitude, and q₀ = 2πr_loop is species-dependent
- **Location:** lines 626–631 ("Because the pump clock is the medium's, electron–muon and electron–proton Coulomb interactions are as static and *as strong* as electron–electron ones, as observed"); cf. lines 444, 518–519 (q₀ = 2πr_loop, r_loop = ℏ/m_ec); Open Questions line 1070–1072.
- **Why wrong:** The universal clock Ω = m_Ac²/ℏ solves the *frequency-mismatch* problem (unequal zitter clocks would time-average cross-species coupling to zero) — that part is fine. It does nothing for *amplitude*. With q₀ = 2πr_loop and r_loop ∝ 1/m_species, a muon's charge quantum would be m_e/m_μ ≈ 1/207 of the electron's, making the μ–μ Coulomb force (m_e/m_μ)² ≈ 2.34×10⁻⁵ of e–e and the e–μ force ~1/207 of e–e. Observed charge equality holds to better than 10⁻⁹ (e.g. muonium/hydrogen spectroscopy, neutrality of matter). For the proton the picture is worse still (composite, r_loop undefined). The words "as strong ... as observed" therefore assert precisely what is unproven.
- **Internal contradiction:** the paper's own Open Questions item 2 (lines 1063–1073) lists "the species-inheritance mechanism for the charge quantum" as *remaining first-principles work* — directly conceding that the strength-universality claimed as a result at line 631 is open.
- **Fix direction:** Restrict line 631 to frequency/staticness ("as static as"), and explicitly flag amplitude universality as open, pointing to the q₀-as-universal-medium-constant candidate resolution. Anything less misstates the theory's current standing on one of the most precisely tested facts in physics.

### M3. §1.7–1.8 Curie Temperature / Paramagnetism — thermal mechanism attributed to Aether temperature contradicts the framework's own thermal sector by ~3 orders of magnitude
- **Location:** lines 269–312 ("heat is the thermal energy of Aether — the random kinetic motion of Aether particles"; "As temperature rises, the thermal energy of the surrounding Aether increases... At the Curie temperature, the thermal disruption is strong enough...").
- **Why wrong (internally):** Elsewhere in the series (Papers 6/6a/6b, and this paper's own §1 lines 94–102) the Aether's normal/thermal component is (a) nearly decoupled from matter energetically (suppression P_th/K_A ~ 10⁻³⁸, quoted at line 97 — ratio 6×10⁻¹⁰/10²⁸ = 6×10⁻³⁸ ✓) and (b) locally at a temperature tied to the CMB scale (~3 K), not to the lab temperature of a heated sample. Heating iron to its Curie point (1043 K) heats the lattice and electron system, not the Aether; and if it did heat the Aether, the 10⁻³⁸ coupling suppression would forbid that thermal bath from "buffeting the electron vortices." As written, the mechanism predicts Curie temperatures set by the Aether's ~K-scale temperature, wrong by orders of magnitude, and contradicts the transparency argument in this paper's own opening section.
- **Fix direction:** Attribute the disruption to the thermal motion of the *electrons/lattice* (which carry the vortices), i.e. thermal agitation of the vortex carriers, not of the medium. The standard k_BT_C ≈ exchange-energy balance then maps cleanly onto the model's exchange story in §1.6.

### M4. §1.10 Meissner Effect — "nothing to couple to" predicts transparency, not expulsion; the mechanism also wrongly generalizes to all paired-electron materials
- **Location:** lines 335–357 ("An external magnetic field... cannot penetrate this region because there are no individual, unbalanced wave sources for the external vorticity to couple to. The external field is forced around the material instead").
- **Why wrong:** (1) Logical inversion: a region containing nothing that couples to the field is, by definition, transparent to it (vacuum is maximally "zero net coherent wave field," and fields fill it freely). Expulsion requires an *active* response — in real superconductors, screening supercurrents (London rigidity, B decaying as e^{−z/λ_L}); an absence of response cannot push flux out. (2) Counterexample from the model's own premise: filled-shell core electrons in ordinary metals, and all electrons in closed-shell diamagnets, are equally "paired with canceling emissions," yet magnetic fields penetrate normal metals and insulators essentially unimpeded (DC). The same lead sample above and below 7.2 K has the same paired core electrons; only the condensate's rigidity changes. The stated mechanism cannot distinguish the two phases. (3) The penetration-depth story ("attenuated by interaction with the Cooper pair condensate," line 355) contradicts the no-coupling claim two sentences earlier — attenuation *is* coupling.
- **Fix direction:** Recast the Meissner section around a coherent condensate *response* (the Cooper-pair condensate as a phase-rigid superfluid that generates counterflow expelling external vorticity — which is actually very natural in this framework), not around absence of sources.

### M5. §1.1 Basic Mechanism (+ §1.6 first paragraph) — the unqualified spin-spin sign rule gets the strongest geometry backwards and contradicts the ferromagnetism paragraph built on it
- **Location:** lines 106–122 ("alike-spinning electrons repel, and opposite-spinning electrons attract... effect is greatest at the poles... naturally producing the strongest forces along the spin axis"); lines 228–233 (ferromagnetic alignment "energetically favorable because antiparallel-spinning neighbors create low-pressure channels between them").
- **Why wrong:**
  1. **Geometry-dependence ignored, with the wrong sign in the highlighted geometry.** Real magnetic dipole-dipole energy: U = (μ₀/4π)[m₁·m₂ − 3(m₁·r̂)(m₂·r̂)]/r³. Side-by-side parallel moments repel (✓ matches the paper's rule), but *coaxial* parallel moments — the along-the-spin-axis configuration the paper singles out as strongest — attract (U = −2μ₀m²/4πr³, the strongest attractive configuration; this is how bar magnets stack N–S). The paper's combination "parallel spins repel" + "greatest along the spin axis" therefore predicts the opposite of observed magnetostatics in exactly the configuration it emphasizes.
  2. **Kinematic slip:** "greater surface area of spin interacts at higher velocities" at the poles — for any rotating structure, surface speed v = ωr sinθ is *minimum* (zero) at the poles and maximum at the equator.
  3. **Self-contradiction in §1.6:** ferromagnetic domains have *parallel* spins (line 228–229), yet the energetic justification offered is that *antiparallel* neighbors attract (lines 230–232). If antiparallel neighbors lower the energy, the ground state is antiferromagnetic. The subsection's own later (and correct) exchange/Pauli/Coulomb explanation (lines 240–264) supersedes this, but the contradictory fluid paragraph remains.
- **Fix direction:** Make the sign rule geometry-resolved (parallel side-by-side repel, coaxial parallel attract — the vortex model can actually reproduce this, since co-rotating side-by-side flows clash at the midplane while coaxial co-rotating flows are cooperative), fix the polar-velocity claim, and delete or rewrite lines 230–233 so §1.6 rests solely on the exchange mechanism.

- **Location:** lines 85–90 ("These are Bernoulli (dynamic) pressure differentials... making Bernoulli dynamics the natural description of **all** electromagnetic forces"); lines 1151–1156 ("The electromagnetic forces described in this paper arise from Bernoulli (dynamic) pressure — the redistribution of kinetic and static energy in flowing Aether").

- **Fix direction:** Restrict the Bernoulli claims to the magnetic/vortex-interaction sector, and describe the electric sector as spin-phase gradient energy (or "effective counterflow kinetic energy" if the spin-orbit effective-velocity reading is intended — but then say so).

### Mo4. Induction mechanisms require nearby matter; real (and the paper's own) vacuum fields do not

- **Issue:** Faraday induction and displacement current operate in matter-free vacuum (e.g. the paper's own self-regenerating vacuum EM wave, lines 852–867, where E and B convert into each other with no bound rings present). The vacuum case needs the medium-level statement (the condensate's own wave equation couples the two aspects), which the EM-waves subsection already contains — the induction bullets should be brought into line with it.

### Mo5. Linearity/time-dilation/Fresnel support claims overreach (inherited from Paper 2)
- **Location:** lines 986–992 ("experimentally confirmed to be perfectly linear... confirmed by velocity time dilation (to 10⁻⁸ precision across velocities from 10⁻⁶c to 0.9994c) and... the Fresnel drag coefficient (exact 1 − 1/n²...)"); repeated at lines 1157–1164.
- **Issues:** (a) The ~10⁻⁸ (and better) time-dilation precision belongs to low-to-mid-β ion experiments (β ≈ 0.03–0.34); at β = 0.9994 (muon storage rings) the verification is at the ~10⁻³ level. (b) The Fresnel drag coefficient is not "exact 1 − 1/n²": the measured coefficient includes the Lorentz dispersion term −(λ/n)(dn/dλ), confirmed by Zeeman's experiments; "exact" overstates. (c) "Perfectly linear... experimentally confirmed" is a theory-laden reading of SR tests. These claims are imported from Paper 2 and were flagged in its review; they should be corrected series-wide together.

### Mo6. Diamagnetism claimed universal "because all atoms have electron pairs"
- **Location:** lines 315–333, esp. 331–332.
- **Issue:** Hydrogen (one unpaired electron) is a counterexample to "all atoms have electron pairs," yet atomic hydrogen still has a diamagnetic susceptibility contribution (Langevin term ∝ ⟨r²⟩ per electron, paired or not); diamagnetism in standard physics is an induced-current (Lenz) response of *each* electron orbital, requiring no pairing. The pair-based mechanism as stated predicts zero diamagnetic response from unpaired-electron species and so gets the universality it advertises from an assumption that is itself not universal.

### Mo8. Static-field permeation analogy inconsistent with its own physics
