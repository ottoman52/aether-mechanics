## Critical Findings

### C1. The spin-force law assumes an instantaneous (quasi-static) field; at physical separations the pumps are deep in the radiation zone, where the far-zone force law is undetermined pending a lock-selection derivation
*(Revised 2026-07-07 after cross-session verification: original "gives a sign-oscillating force" was overstated — that outcome holds only under naive global-phase synchronization. Criticality unchanged.)*
- **Location:** §10.3 (eq:spin-force, TeX 820–847), §10.4 (eq:pump-clock, 849–890), interacting with flag 6 (1029–1033).
- **Claim:** Two synchronized pumps q_i(t) = q₀sin(±ωt) interact via potential-theory energy E = K_φq₁q₂/(4πd), giving time-averaged F = K_φq₀²/(8πd²) with a distance-independent sign rule ("like signs repel").
- **Why wrong:** The potential-theory interaction is exact only for an incompressible (infinite-propagation-speed) field. Flag 6 states the spin (magnon) branch propagates at c, and §10.4 clocks the pump at Ω = m_Ac²/ℏ. The field wavelength is λ_Ω = 2πc/Ω = 1.86×10⁻¹⁵ m. Every physically relevant separation is enormously non-compact: the Bohr radius is 2.8×10⁴ λ_Ω; even the classical electron radius is ~1.5 λ_Ω. The outcome at kr ≫ 1 then depends on what "synchronized" means, and §10.3 never says: (i) under **global-phase synchronization** (equal phases in a common frame — the natural reading of the text), the time-averaged product is ⟨q₁(t)q₂(t−d/c)⟩ = (q₀²/2)cos(Ωd/c) and the force ∝ cos(Ωd/c)/d² — sign oscillates every ~10⁻¹⁵ m; (ii) under **local 0/π locking to the arriving field** — the revised Paper 4 §Falloff argument, verified numerically (F·r² constant to six digits out to kr = 100) — the quadrature/radiative term cancels and a clean fixed-sign 1/r² survives at all distances. So the far-zone force is **undetermined pending lock selection**, and the lock-selection question is itself nontrivial: in the mutual delay-coupled problem the symmetric locked branch has kd-dependent local offsets, and free energy-relaxation of the phases favors universal *attraction* — local 0/π selection must therefore come from the topological/DC structure of the winding pump, which is underived (this is flag 4's content, escalated: flag 4 assumes the interaction form rather than deriving the lock). 7(a)'s specific failure is that it never invokes Paper 4's conditional argument at the force-law step — the quasi-static form is presented unconditionally. A further internal tension: the static field-line/Gauss language presumes a DC component of the pump field, but the §10.4 pump is zero-mean sinusoidal — rectification is nowhere derived. Flag 5 (isolated-charge energetics) does not cover any of this (it is about radiative loss of one charge, not the two-charge retarded force).
- **Evidence/recomputation:** λ_Ω = 2πℏ/(m_Ac) = 1.862×10⁻¹⁵ m at m_A=666 MeV; a₀/λ_Ω = 28,416; 2πr_loop/λ_Ω = m_A/m_e = 1303 (the source itself spans ~1300 wavelengths — see M3). Local-lock limit verified numerically in a companion session: receiver locked at 0/π to the locally arriving field gives F·r² constant to six digits out to kr = 100.
- **Fix direction:** Immediate (accepted disposition): (c) demote eq:spin-force and eq:k-final from "derivation" to "near-zone normalization, conditional on local 0/π lock," add this as honest flag #7, and cross-reference Paper 4 §Falloff's conditional locked-response argument at the force-law step. Real closure (co-equal top open item with the 2/π duty integral): derive lock selection — either (a) a rectification mechanism giving the pump a DC monopole component (a pure sinusoid has none), or (b) show the delay-coupled two-pump dynamics selects the 0/π branch from the topological/DC structure. Until one of these is done, the headline claim "Coulomb's constant derived" is conditional — what stands unconditionally is the near-zone normalization and the dimensional combination ke² = ρ_Aκ_e²r_loop²/(8π) (which is genuinely striking numerically).

## Moderate Findings

### Mo1. The sign rule ("like signs repel") is imported from a different analogy than the one used to compute the magnitude
- **Location:** §10.3, TeX 838–847.
- The magnitude comes from the incompressible-source analogy (E_int = K_φq₁q₂/4πd, "mathematically identical to potential flow"). But in that analogy, physically realized pulsating/steady fluid sources of like sign in phase **attract** (secondary Bjerknes; momentum-flux argument) — force = −∂E/∂d at fixed boundary conditions. The repulsion quoted is the vortex-gas rule, valid for *constrained topological* charges where the force is +∂E/∂d (fixed-q, like fixed-current magnets). Which constraint class the winding pumps belong to is the entire content of Coulomb's sign structure, and the paper asserts rather than derives it (one sentence, "the vortex-gas rule"). Since q is topologically quantized, the fixed-q convention is defensible — but the step deserves a derivation or at minimum an honest flag, since the two standard conventions give opposite signs.

### Mo3. "Topological charge ⇒ F(Q²) = 1 exactly" is presented as a "Confirmed Resolution" but is a contested postulate

### Mo4. The "single postulate, two observables" packaging overstates the identity of the two normalizations
- **Location:** §10.4 (873–879), §11 "What This Does Not Determine" (1157–1182), Status (1206–1216).
- The magneton input fixes a *period* on the **azimuthal** clock (one charge passes per T_C = 2π/ω_C); the Coulomb input fixes an *amplitude* (q₀ = 2πr_loop, a length) for a pump running on the **poloidal** clock Ω = 1303 ω_C. Both are verbally "one winding per cycle," but they are statements about different quantities on different clocks; the claim that a normalization "adjusted by hand to fit k would have no reason to land the magneton" is weaker than presented, because the two calculations do not literally share a parameter — they share a slogan. The pairing is suggestive and worth keeping, but should be described as two analogous normalizations of the same winding, not one postulate.
